## Legal Case Analysis Report: Inga, 87 years old

### Case Summary
Inga, an 87-year-old woman, lives alone in her house in the countryside. She is exhibiting signs of declining capacity, including forgetting medication, neglecting cooking and bill payments, and experiencing falls. Neighbors have raised concerns. Inga insists she is fine and rejects the idea of external assistance in her home.

### Applicable Law & Legal Framework
*   **Socialtjänstlagen (SoL) 2001:453 [Ref 1]:** This law governs social services provided by municipalities in Sweden.
    *   **2 kap. 1 §:** Municipalities have the ultimate responsibility to ensure individuals receive necessary support and assistance [Ref 1].
    *   **3 kap. 1 §:** Social services responsibilities include understanding living conditions, promoting good environments, informing about social services, proactively promoting good living conditions, and providing care, service, advice, support, and financial assistance [Ref 1].
    *   **3 kap. 6 §:** Social services should facilitate home living and social contact through home care, day activities, or similar services [Ref 1].
    *   **4 kap. 1 §:** Individuals unable to meet their needs themselves or through other means are entitled to assistance from social services for their sustenance and overall living [Ref 1].
    *   **4 kap. 2 a §:** Social services can offer home care to elderly individuals without prior needs assessment [Ref 1].
    *   **4 kap. 2 b §:** Individuals receiving home care under 4 kap. 1 or 2 a § should be offered a designated care contact, unless clearly unnecessary [Ref 1].
    *   **5 kap. 4 §:** Social care for the elderly should focus on enabling a dignified life and well-being (the "value base") [Ref 1].
    *   **5 kap. 5 §:** Social services should work to ensure good housing for the elderly and provide support and assistance in the home and other easily accessible services to those who need it [Ref 1].
    *   **11 kap. 1 §:** Social services must promptly investigate any information received that may warrant action by the board [Ref 1].
*   **Hälso- och sjukvårdslagen (HSL) 2017:30 [Ref 2]:** This law governs healthcare in Sweden.
    *   **5 kap. 1 §:** Healthcare activities must meet the requirements for good care, including good quality, safety, respect for self-determination and integrity, and accessibility [Ref 2].
    *   **12 kap. 2 §:** Municipalities may offer healthcare in the home (home healthcare) in ordinary housing and in special housing forms [Ref 2].
    *   **16 kap. 4 §:** When an individual needs both healthcare and social services, the region and municipality must create an individual plan with the individual's consent [Ref 2].

### Legal Analysis
Inga's case presents a conflict between her right to self-determination and the municipality's duty to provide care and prevent harm.

1.  **Initial Assessment and Investigation:** The municipality has a responsibility to investigate the concerns raised by the neighbors [Ref 1, 11 kap. 1 §]. This investigation should involve contacting Inga, visiting her home, and assessing her current situation. The municipality should proceed carefully and respectfully, explaining the purpose of the visit and emphasizing their desire to support her well-being.
2.  **Needs Assessment:** The municipality needs to conduct a thorough needs assessment to determine the extent of Inga's needs. This assessment should cover her ability to manage medication, prepare meals, pay bills, maintain her home, and her risk of falling [Ref 1, 4 kap. 1 §].
3.  **Offer of Services:** Even without a full needs assessment, the municipality can offer Inga home care services [Ref 1, 4 kap. 2 a §]. This could include assistance with medication management, meal preparation, cleaning, and personal care.
4.  **Inga's Refusal:** Inga has the right to refuse services [Ref 2, 5 kap. 1 §]. However, the municipality must ensure that her refusal is informed and voluntary. This means explaining the potential risks of refusing care and ensuring she understands the consequences of her decisions. The municipality should also explore the reasons behind her refusal and address any concerns she may have.
5.  **Individual Plan:** If Inga requires both healthcare and social care, the municipality, together with the regional healthcare provider, should offer to create an individual plan [Ref 2, 16 kap. 4 §]. This plan requires Inga's consent.
6.  **Risk Assessment:** The municipality must assess the risks associated with Inga's current situation. Her forgetting medication, neglecting cooking and bill payments, and experiencing falls all indicate a significant risk to her health and safety.
7.  **Capacity Assessment:** If there are concerns about Inga's cognitive capacity, the municipality may need to involve a medical professional to assess her ability to understand information and make decisions about her care.
8.  **Guardianship (God man/Förvaltare):** If Inga lacks the capacity to make informed decisions and is at risk of harm, the municipality may need to consider applying for guardianship [Gaps/Unknowns: Information on guardianship application process is missing]. This is a legal process that involves appointing a guardian to make decisions on Inga's behalf. This is a serious step and should only be taken as a last resort, after all other options have been explored.
9.  **Documentation:** All interactions with Inga, assessments, and decisions made should be carefully documented [Gaps/Unknowns: Specific documentation requirements are missing].

### Findings / Recommendations
The municipality should take the following steps:

1.  **Contact Inga and schedule a home visit:** Explain the concerns raised by neighbors and the municipality's desire to support her well-being [Ref 1, 11 kap. 1 §].
2.  **Conduct a comprehensive needs assessment:** Assess Inga's ability to manage her daily life and the risks she faces [Ref 1, 4 kap. 1 §].
3.  **Offer home care services:** Even without a full needs assessment, offer assistance with medication management, meal preparation, and other tasks [Ref 1, 4 kap. 2 a §].
4.  **Ensure Inga's refusal is informed and voluntary:** Explain the potential risks of refusing care and address any concerns she may have [Ref 2, 5 kap. 1 §].
5.  **Offer an individual plan:** If Inga requires both healthcare and social care, offer to create an individual plan in collaboration with the regional healthcare provider [Ref 2, 16 kap. 4 §].
6.  **Assess Inga's cognitive capacity:** If there are concerns about her ability to make informed decisions, involve a medical professional.
7.  **Consider guardianship as a last resort:** If Inga lacks capacity and is at risk of harm, explore the possibility of applying for guardianship [Gaps/Unknowns: Information on guardianship application process is missing].
8.  **Document all interactions and decisions:** Maintain detailed records of all assessments, offers of services, and Inga's responses [Gaps/Unknowns: Specific documentation requirements are missing].

**Risks of Action:** Applying for guardianship against Inga's will could be seen as a violation of her right to self-determination.
**Risks of Inaction:** Failing to provide support could lead to further deterioration of Inga's health and safety, potentially resulting in serious injury or death. The municipality could be held liable for negligence if it fails to take reasonable steps to protect Inga.
