## Topic
- Europe VAT updates

## Document Metadata
- Jurisdiction/scope: Europe, including EU member states and some non-EU countries like Ukraine and the UK [Ref 1][Ref 7][Ref 8].

## Requirements / Specifications
- **EN 16931 E-Invoicing Standard:**
  - Defines the semantic data model for core elements of an e-invoice [Ref 4].
  - Technical formats: UBL (Universal Business Language) and UN/CEFACT CII (Cross Industry Invoice) [Ref 4].
  - Components: header information, seller details, buyer details, invoice line items, tax details, totals and summaries, and payment terms and information [Ref 4].
  - Variations exist as CIUS (Core Invoice Usage Specification) and Extensions [Ref 4].

## Tax & VAT Compliance
- **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM):**
  - Aims to prevent carbon leakage by imposing carbon costs on imports from countries with less stringent emission regulations [Ref 12].
  - Applies to sectors such as cement, iron and steel, aluminum, fertilizers, electricity, and hydrogen [Ref 12].
- **EU VAT Directive Article 19 (Transfer of Going Concern - TOGC):**
  - Requires the transferee to be entitled to deduct input VAT for the TOGC exemption to apply in Sweden [Ref 5].
- **Sweden:**
  - Government plans to cut food VAT from 12% to 6% starting April 1, 2026, until January 1, 2028 [Ref 9].
  - Clarification on VAT place of supply rules for EV charging: electricity supply is where consumption occurs; network access fee is where the customer resides [Ref 18].
- **UK:**
  - Debate on VAT registration threshold: potential increase to £100,000 or decrease to £30,000 [Ref 8].
- **Germany:**
  - B2B e-invoicing mandate with phased rollout [Ref 7].
    - Phase 1: Businesses must receive structured e-invoices from January 1, 2025 [Ref 7].
    - Phase 2: Large businesses issue e-invoices by 2027, all businesses by 2028 [Ref 7].
  - Uses XRechnung for B2G e-invoicing [Ref 7].
- **Poland:**
  - Amendments to the VAT Act and the KSeF Act introduce transitional provisions and technical updates to the mandatory e-invoicing regime [Ref 17].
  - Phased use of traditional invoicing allowed for businesses meeting specific thresholds [Ref 17]:
    - February 1 – March 31, 2026: Taxpayers with total 2024 sales and VAT not exceeding PLN 200 million may issue invoices in paper or standard electronic form [Ref 17].
    - April 1 – December 31, 2026: Taxpayers may use traditional invoicing if their monthly total sales and tax remain below PLN 10,000 [Ref 17].
    - February 1 – December 31, 2026: Businesses may issue paper or electronic invoices linked to cash register receipts, provided they comply with fiscal cash register requirements [Ref 17].
- **Greece:**
  - Mandatory e-invoicing for specific business transactions [Ref 13].
  - Applies to domestic B2B transactions, transactions with third countries (excluding retail sales), and public sector dealings [Ref 13].
  - All e-invoices must comply with the European e-invoicing standard (EN 16931) [Ref 13].
  - Incentives for early adoption include full amortization of initial costs and increased deductible expenses for the first year [Ref 13].
  - Transactions for which electronic invoicing is exclusively issued are determined [Ref 13].
  - If entities choose electronic invoicing before its mandatory application, the expense for the initial procurement of technical equipment and software required for the application of electronic invoicing is fully depreciated for purposes of the Income Tax Code (ITC) in the year of its realization, increased by one hundred percent (100%) [Ref 13].
  - The expense for the production, transmission and electronic archiving of electronic invoices for the first calendar year of issuing sales documents through electronic invoicing that is recognized for deduction from gross income from business activity, according to article 22 of the ITC, is increased by one hundred percent (100%) in the year of its realization [Ref 13].
- **Ukraine:**
  - Aligning tax legislation with EU Directive 2021/514 (DAC7) and the OECD Model Rules for Reporting by Platform Operators [Ref 1][Ref 16].
  - Reduced individual income tax rate of up to 5% for qualified resident sellers whose annual income is below UAH 6.7 million as of January 1 [Ref 16].
  - Standard rate of 18% will apply to nonqualified sellers [Ref 16].
  - Platform sellers will be exempt from providing additional bank account details if they make no more than three transactions per year and have total sales not exceeding EUR 2,000 annually [Ref 16].

## Implementation Impact
- **EN 16931:** Requires businesses to adapt to country-specific CIUS and Extensions [Ref 4].
- **Germany:** Businesses need to prepare for receiving and issuing e-invoices in a structured format [Ref 7].
- **Greece:** Businesses need to integrate ERP systems with certified e-invoicing providers and train staff [Ref 13].
- **Sweden:** Ruling on TOGC exemption may significantly impact the transfer of VAT-exempt and mixed businesses [Ref 5].
- **HMRC (UK):** Businesses in labor supply chains need to review their arrangements to avoid liabilities related to mini umbrella company fraud [Ref 10].
