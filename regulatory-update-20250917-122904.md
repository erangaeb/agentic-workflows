## Topic
- e-invoice regulatory updates Taiwan.

## Document Metadata
- **Jurisdiction/scope:** Taiwan, applying to foreign and domestic companies providing online sales, advertising, and cloud services to customers in Taiwan [Ref 1]. The regulations also cover internet influencers selling goods or services domestically and domestic buyers of foreign cloud services [Ref 2].
- **Publishing authority:** Taiwan Ministry of Finance [Ref 1][Ref 2].
- **Version/document date:**
    - Ministry of Finance Order No. 11404590640 was published on September 10 [Ref 2].
    - A clarification on tax obligations for cross-border purchases of foreign cloud services was issued on September 11 [Ref 2].
    - An article reporting on these changes is dated September 16, 2025 [Ref 1].
- **Effective/compliance dates (incl. grace periods):** The guidelines and the cloud service clarification took effect immediately upon publication [Ref 2].

## Requirements / Specifications
- **Scope of transactions:** E-invoices must be issued for all online sales, advertising, and cloud service transactions [Ref 1]. This includes both B2B and B2C sales [Ref 1].
- **Mandatory fields:** E-invoices must include [Ref 1]:
    - The buyer’s information.
    - Transaction details.
    - A unique invoice number.

## Tax & VAT Compliance
- **VAT registration:** Foreign suppliers must register for VAT in Taiwan if their annual sales to Taiwanese customers exceed a specified threshold [Ref 1].
- **Reporting frequency:** Monthly VAT returns must be filed electronically [Ref 1].
- **E-invoice obligation:** E-invoices must be issued for each taxable transaction [Ref 1].
- **Online sales and influencer obligations:** Tax registration is required for internet influencers selling goods or services domestically if they have a physical business location in China and their monthly internet sales meet the taxable threshold [Ref 2].
- **Tax rates for advertising playback services:**
    - **Domestic digital platforms:**
        - 5% rate for sales of advertising playback services to domestic advertisers, regardless of viewers’ residence [Ref 2].
        - A 1% rate may apply under certain specified conditions [Ref 2].
    - **Sales to overseas advertisers:**
        - 0% rate for advertising playback services provided to non-paying overseas viewers [Ref 2].
        - A 1% rate applies in certain cases [Ref 2].
    - **Overseas digital platforms:**
        - 5% rate for sales of advertising playback services to overseas advertisers when viewed by non-paying domestic viewers [Ref 2].
- **Cloud services and generative AI:** Domestic buyers of foreign cloud services (including generative AI and cloud storage) from nonresident sellers without a fixed place of business in Taiwan must report and pay business tax, unless they are [Ref 2]:
    - An individual.
    - A specialized business calculating tax in a prescribed way.
    - Selling specified services to schools or research institutions.
- **Tax reporting deadline for cloud services:** Within 15 days of purchase, unless exceptions apply [Ref 2].

## Submission & Interfaces
- **Submission channel:** E-invoices must be submitted to the tax authorities in real time via the government’s electronic platform [Ref 1].
- **Tax filing for cloud services:** Can be done via the Ministry of Finance’s online portal or by submitting the prescribed tax payment form manually [Ref 2].

## Changes vs. Previous
- The new rules are part of Taiwan’s ongoing efforts to modernize its tax system and address the challenges of the digital economy [Ref 1].
- These are new tax regulations affecting online sales, advertising, and cloud services [Ref 1].

## Implementation Impact
- Businesses affected by the new regulations should [Ref 1]:
    - Review their invoicing and tax reporting processes to ensure compliance.
    - Register for VAT in Taiwan if required.
    - Implement systems to generate and submit e-invoices electronically.
    - Monitor updates from Taiwan’s Ministry of Finance for further guidance.
- **Penalties:** Failure to comply with the new requirements may result in fines and administrative penalties [Ref 1].
