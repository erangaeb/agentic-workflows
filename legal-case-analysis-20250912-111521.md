## Legal Case Analysis Report: Inga, 87 years old

### Case Summary
Inga, an 87-year-old woman, lives alone in her house in the countryside. She is experiencing difficulties with medication management, cooking, bill payment, and has a history of falls. Neighbors have raised concerns about her well-being. Inga maintains that she is fine and does not want external assistance in her home.

### Applicable Law & Legal Framework
*   **Socialtjänstlagen (SoL) 2001:453** [Ref 1]: This law governs social services in Sweden, including elder care.
    *   **2 kap. 1 §:** Municipal responsibility for social services and ensuring individuals receive necessary support [Ref 1].
    *   **3 kap. 1 §:** Social services tasks including monitoring living conditions, promoting good living conditions, informing about social services, and providing care and support [Ref 1].
    *   **3 kap. 6 §:** The social services should facilitate the possibility for individuals to live at home through home care or similar services [Ref 1].
    *   **4 kap. 1 §:** Right to assistance for those who cannot meet their needs themselves [Ref 1].
    *   **4 kap. 2 a §:** Social services can offer home care to elderly without prior needs assessment [Ref 1].
    *   **5 kap. 4 §:** Focus on a dignified life and well-being for the elderly (the "värdegrund") [Ref 1].
    *   **5 kap. 5 §:** Promoting independent living in secure conditions, providing support and help in the home, and establishing special housing for those in need [Ref 1].
*   **Hälso- och sjukvårdslagen (HSL) 2017:30** [Ref 2]: This law governs healthcare in Sweden.
    *   **5 kap. 1 §:** Healthcare activities must meet the requirements for good care, including good quality, patient safety, respect for self-determination and integrity [Ref 2].
    *   **12 kap. 2 §:** Municipalities can offer healthcare at home (hemsjukvård) [Ref 2].
    *   **16 kap. 4 §:** Requirement for an individual plan when a person needs both healthcare and social services [Ref 2].

### Legal Analysis
Inga's case presents a conflict between her right to self-determination and the municipality's duty to ensure her well-being.

1.  **Initial Assessment:** The municipality has a responsibility to be aware of the living conditions of its residents (SoL 3 kap. 1 §) [Ref 1]. The neighbors' concerns and Inga's reported difficulties (forgetting medication, falls) trigger the municipality's obligation to investigate her situation.
2.  **Offering Support:** The municipality can offer Inga home care services without a prior needs assessment (SoL 4 kap. 2 a §) [Ref 1]. This allows for a proactive approach to address her potential needs.
3.  **Inga's Refusal:** Inga's refusal of assistance is a key factor. Under HSL 5 kap. 1 §, healthcare should respect the patient's self-determination [Ref 2]. However, this right is not absolute. The municipality must assess whether Inga's decision-making capacity is impaired due to her age or health conditions.
4.  **Needs Assessment:** Despite Inga's initial refusal, the municipality should conduct a thorough needs assessment to determine the extent of her difficulties and risks. This assessment should consider her physical and cognitive abilities, her living environment, and her social network.
5.  **Individual Plan:** If the needs assessment reveals that Inga requires both healthcare and social services, the municipality, together with the relevant healthcare provider (region), must create an individual plan (HSL 16 kap. 4 §) [Ref 2]. This plan should outline the necessary interventions and responsibilities of each party, with Inga's consent if possible.
6.  **Capacity Assessment:** If there are concerns about Inga's capacity to make informed decisions, a formal capacity assessment should be conducted by qualified healthcare professionals. This assessment will determine whether Inga understands the risks associated with her refusal of care and can make rational decisions about her well-being.
7.  **Involuntary Intervention:** Involuntary intervention is a last resort and requires a legal basis. If Inga lacks the capacity to make informed decisions and her life or health is at serious risk, the municipality may need to consider applying for guardianship ("god man" or "förvaltare") through the court. A guardian can then make decisions on Inga's behalf, including consenting to necessary care. The legal framework for guardianship is found in the Föräldrabalken (FB).
8.  **Risk Mitigation:** Even if involuntary intervention is not immediately necessary, the municipality should implement strategies to mitigate the risks to Inga's health and safety. This could include:
    *   Regular home visits to monitor her condition.
    *   Collaboration with her neighbors to provide informal support and report any concerns.
    *   Encouraging her to use assistive technology, such as a medication dispenser or a personal alarm.

### Findings / Recommendations
The municipality should take the following steps:

1.  **Contact Inga and offer home care services (SoL 4 kap. 2 a §) [Ref 1], emphasizing the benefits and addressing her concerns about privacy.**
2.  **Conduct a comprehensive needs assessment, focusing on her ability to manage medication, nutrition, finances, and personal safety.**
3.  **If the needs assessment reveals significant risks, arrange for a formal capacity assessment by qualified healthcare professionals.**
4.  **If Inga is deemed to lack capacity and is at serious risk, initiate the process of applying for guardianship through the court.**
5.  **Develop an individual plan (HSL 16 kap. 4 §) [Ref 2] in collaboration with healthcare providers, outlining necessary interventions and responsibilities.**
6.  **Implement risk mitigation strategies, such as regular home visits and collaboration with neighbors.**
7.  **Document all interactions, assessments, and decisions thoroughly.**

**Risks of Action:** Legal challenges from Inga or her family if interventions are perceived as infringing on her rights.
**Risks of Inaction:** Inga's health and safety could deteriorate, leading to serious injury or death. The municipality could be held liable for failing to fulfill its duty of care.
