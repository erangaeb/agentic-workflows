## Denmark's E-Invoicing Regulatory Landscape: Recent Updates and Mandates

Denmark has consistently been at the forefront of digital public administration, with a robust e-invoicing infrastructure in place for over two decades. While e-invoicing has been mandatory for Business-to-Government (B2G) transactions for many years, recent legislative changes, particularly the **new Danish Bookkeeping Act (Bogføringsloven)**, are significantly expanding the scope of digital transaction requirements to the Business-to-Business (B2B) sector.

### 1. The Major Update: New Danish Bookkeeping Act (Bogføringsloven)

The most significant recent regulatory update impacting e-invoicing in Denmark is the **new Bookkeeping Act (Bogføringsloven)**, which came into force on **July 1, 2022** [1]. This act introduces a phased mandate for digital bookkeeping, which inherently includes requirements for digital invoicing for certain businesses.

**Key Provisions and Impact:**

*   **Mandatory Digital Bookkeeping:** The act mandates that companies must use a digital bookkeeping system that meets specific requirements, including the ability to send and receive e-invoices in structured formats.
*   **E-invoicing as a Component:** While not a direct e-invoicing mandate for *all* B2B transactions, the requirement for digital bookkeeping systems to handle structured invoices effectively pushes businesses towards e-invoicing for their sales and purchase processes.
*   **Structured Formats:** The act emphasizes the use of structured electronic formats for invoices, with **Peppol BIS Billing 3.0** being explicitly highlighted as the preferred standard [2].
*   **Phased Implementation:** The mandate is being rolled out gradually based on company size:
    *   **July 1, 2022:** The new act came into force.
    *   **January 1, 2024:** Companies with an annual turnover exceeding DKK 3 million (approx. €400,000) must comply with the digital bookkeeping requirements [3].
    *   **January 1, 2026:** Companies with an annual turnover of DKK 3 million or less must comply [3].
*   **Scope:** This mandate primarily targets Danish-registered companies. Foreign companies without a permanent establishment in Denmark are generally exempt from the specific bookkeeping system requirements but may still need to comply if they transact with Danish entities subject to the act.

### 2. Existing Framework: NemHandel and Peppol

Denmark's e-invoicing infrastructure is built upon two primary pillars:

*   **NemHandel:** Launched in 2005, NemHandel is Denmark's national e-business infrastructure. It facilitates the secure and reliable exchange of electronic documents, including e-invoices, between businesses and public authorities. It operates as a network of certified access points [4].
    *   **OIOUBL:** The primary e-invoice format used within the NemHandel network has traditionally been **OIOUBL (Offentlig Information Online Universal Business Language)**, a Danish customization of the UBL (Universal Business Language) standard [5].
*   **Peppol (Pan-European Public Procurement Online):** Denmark has been a strong advocate and early adopter of Peppol. The Danish Agency for Digital Government (Digitaliseringsstyrelsen) acts as the Peppol Authority for Denmark [6].
    *   **Growing Importance:** While OIOUBL remains in use, Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 is increasingly becoming the preferred and recommended standard, especially with the new Bookkeeping Act, due to its interoperability across Europe and beyond. Danish public entities are reachable via Peppol [7].

### 3. Mandatory vs. Voluntary E-Invoicing

*   **Business-to-Government (B2G):** E-invoicing has been mandatory for all invoices sent to Danish public sector entities since **2005** [8]. This predates and aligns with the later **EU Directive 2014/55/EU** on electronic invoicing in public procurement, which mandated e-invoicing for public sector contracts across the EU [9].
*   **Business-to-Business (B2B) & Business-to-Consumer (B2C):**
    *   Historically, e-invoicing in the B2B and B2C sectors was largely **voluntary**, driven by efficiency gains and industry best practices.
    *   The **new Bookkeeping Act** marks a significant shift, making digital bookkeeping (and by extension, structured e-invoicing for relevant transactions) **mandatory** for a large segment of Danish businesses based on their turnover.

### 4. Technical Standards and Formats

*   **OIOUBL:** Remains a valid and widely used format within the NemHandel network, particularly for legacy systems and existing B2G connections.
*   **Peppol BIS Billing 3.0:** Increasingly the recommended and preferred standard for new implementations and for compliance with the new Bookkeeping Act. It offers greater international interoperability.
*   **Other Formats:** While other formats (e.g., PDF, XML without specific structure) might be exchanged, they generally do not meet the "structured electronic format" requirements for mandatory digital bookkeeping.

### 5. Benefits and Implications for Businesses

The regulatory updates, particularly the new Bookkeeping Act, aim to:

*   **Enhance Digitalization:** Further accelerate the digital transformation of Danish businesses.
*   **Improve Efficiency:** Reduce manual processes, data entry errors, and processing times for invoices.
*   **Increase Transparency and Compliance:** Provide tax authorities with better insights into business transactions, reducing tax evasion and improving overall compliance.
*   **Reduce Costs:** Long-term savings on printing, postage, and manual handling.
*   **Boost Interoperability:** Promote the use of international standards like Peppol, facilitating cross-border trade.

**Implications for Businesses:**

*   **System Adaptation:** Businesses must ensure their accounting and ERP systems are capable of generating, sending, receiving, and processing e-invoices in the required structured formats (primarily Peppol BIS Billing 3.0).
*   **Service Providers:** Many businesses will likely rely on e-invoicing service providers (Access Points) to connect to the NemHandel and Peppol networks.
*   **Training:** Staff will need training on new digital bookkeeping processes and e-invoicing workflows.
*   **Compliance Risk:** Non-compliance with the new Bookkeeping Act can lead to penalties.

### 6. Future Outlook

Denmark's trajectory is clear: a continued push towards comprehensive digitalization of business processes. While there are no immediate plans for a real-time reporting system (like VAT e-reporting in some other EU countries), the foundation laid by the new Bookkeeping Act and the strong emphasis on structured e-invoicing could pave the way for such developments in the future. The focus remains on leveraging digital tools to enhance efficiency, reduce administrative burdens, and ensure robust tax compliance.
