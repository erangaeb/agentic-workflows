## Legal Case Analysis Report: Inga, 87 years old

### Case Summary
Inga, an 87-year-old woman, lives alone in her house in the countryside. She is experiencing memory issues, forgetting medication, neglecting cooking and bill payments, and has fallen several times without seeking help. Neighbors have raised concerns. Inga insists she is fine and does not want assistance in her home.

### Applicable Law & Legal Framework
*   **Socialtjänstlagen (SoL) 2001:453 [Ref 1]:** This law governs social services in Sweden, including elder care.
    *   **2 kap. 1 §:** Municipalities have the ultimate responsibility to ensure individuals receive the support and assistance they need [Ref 1].
    *   **3 kap. 1 §:** Social services are responsible for being familiar with living conditions in the municipality, promoting good living conditions, informing about social services, and providing care, service, and support to those in need [Ref 1].
    *   **3 kap. 6 §:** Social services should facilitate home-based living and social contact through home care, day activities, or similar services [Ref 1].
    *   **4 kap. 1 §:** Individuals unable to meet their needs themselves are entitled to assistance from social services [Ref 1].
    *   **4 kap. 2 a §:** Social services can offer home care to elderly individuals without prior needs assessment [Ref 1].
    *   **5 kap. 4 §:** Elder care should aim to ensure a dignified life and well-being for older individuals (the "värdegrund") [Ref 1].
    *   **5 kap. 5 §:** Social services should work to provide good housing for the elderly and provide support and assistance in the home and other accessible services to those who need it [Ref 1].
    *   **11 kap. 1 §:** Social services must promptly investigate any information suggesting that action by the municipality may be needed [Ref 1].
*   **Hälso- och sjukvårdslagen (HSL) 2017:30 [Ref 3]:** This law governs healthcare in Sweden.
    *   **5 kap. 1 §:** Healthcare must be of good quality, meet the patient's needs for safety and security, respect the patient's self-determination and integrity, promote good contact between patient and staff, and be easily accessible [Ref 3].
    *   **12 kap. 2 §:** Municipalities may offer healthcare at home (hemsjukvård) in ordinary housing [Ref 3].
    *   **16 kap. 4 §:** When an individual needs both healthcare and social services, the region and municipality should create an individual plan with the individual's consent [Ref 3].
*   **God man/Förvaltare (Guardianship) [Ref 2]:** Appointed by the court to assist individuals who cannot manage their affairs due to illness, mental disorder, or weakened health.
    *   A *god man* (guardian) assists with rights, property, and personal care [Ref 2].
    *   A *förvaltare* (administrator) has broader authority and can make decisions without consent if the person cannot care for themselves [Ref 2].
    *   Application for a *god man* or *förvaltare* can be made by the individual, a close relative, or the *överförmyndare* (municipal guardianship authority) [Ref 2].

### Legal Analysis
1.  **Duty to Investigate:** The municipality has a duty to investigate the neighbors' concerns about Inga's well-being [Ref 1, 11 kap. 1 §]. The reports of falls, medication mismanagement, and neglected bills trigger this obligation.
2.  **Initial Contact and Assessment:** The social services should contact Inga to assess her situation. This should be done sensitively, respecting her autonomy [Ref 3, 5 kap. 1 §]. The initial contact should aim to build trust and understand Inga's perspective.
3.  **Needs Assessment:** A thorough needs assessment is crucial to determine the extent of Inga's needs. This assessment should cover her physical health, cognitive abilities, ability to manage daily tasks, and social situation.
4.  **Voluntary Assistance:** The municipality should first explore voluntary solutions. This could include offering home care services (hemtjänst) without a full needs assessment [Ref 1, 4 kap. 2 a §], assistance with medication management, meal delivery, or help with paying bills.
5.  **Inga's Refusal:** Inga's refusal of assistance presents a challenge. While her autonomy must be respected [Ref 3, 5 kap. 1 §], the municipality also has a duty to ensure her well-being [Ref 1, 2 kap. 1 §].
6.  **Risk Assessment:** The municipality must assess the risks associated with Inga's refusal of care. The falls, medication errors, and neglected bills suggest a significant risk to her health and safety.
7.  **Individual Plan:** If Inga consents, the municipality should collaborate with the regional healthcare provider to create an individual plan [Ref 3, 16 kap. 4 §]. This plan should outline the necessary interventions and responsibilities of each party.
8.  **Guardianship (God man/Förvaltare):** If Inga lacks the capacity to understand the risks and benefits of accepting or refusing care, and if less intrusive measures are insufficient, the municipality should consider applying to the court for the appointment of a *god man* or *förvaltare* [Ref 2]. This is a significant step that requires careful consideration and documentation. The *överförmyndare* in the municipality can assist with this process [Ref 2].
9.  **Forced Care:** Forced care is a last resort and requires a court order. It is only justified if Inga poses a serious risk to herself or others and lacks the capacity to make informed decisions. The legal threshold for forced care is very high.

### Findings / Recommendations
1.  **Immediate Action:** The municipality should immediately attempt to contact Inga and conduct a home visit to assess her situation.
2.  **Comprehensive Assessment:** Conduct a comprehensive assessment of Inga's needs, including her physical and cognitive abilities, and her ability to manage daily tasks.
3.  **Offer Voluntary Services:** Offer Inga a range of voluntary services, such as home care, medication management, and meal delivery. Explain the benefits of these services in a clear and respectful manner.
4.  **Document Everything:** Document all interactions with Inga, the assessment findings, and the services offered.
5.  **Risk Mitigation:** Implement risk mitigation strategies, such as installing fall detectors or arranging for regular check-in calls.
6.  **Guardianship Application (If Necessary):** If Inga continues to refuse necessary care and lacks the capacity to make informed decisions, initiate the process of applying for a *god man* or *förvaltare*. Consult with the *överförmyndare* for guidance.
7.  **Collaboration:** Collaborate with healthcare providers, neighbors, and other relevant parties to gather information and coordinate care.
8.  **Legal Procedures:** Ensure all legal procedures are followed, including proper notification and documentation.

**Risks of Action:**
*   Violating Inga's autonomy and right to self-determination if forced care is implemented without proper legal justification.
*   Legal challenges if procedures are not followed correctly.

**Risks of Inaction:**
*   Inga's health and safety could be compromised.
*   The municipality could be held liable for negligence if it fails to provide adequate care.
*   Violation of Inga's right to a dignified life and well-being [Ref 1, 5 kap. 4 §].
