## Format Requirements
- **South Africa:** E-Invoice is defined as a tax invoice in a structured electronic format for automatic processing [Ref 9].
- **EU (ViDA):** Structured e-invoices must follow a common EU format (e.g., EN16931) [Ref 6].
- **Côte d’Ivoire:** E-invoices must include specific mandatory markers, such as a certification QR code and electronic fiscal seal [Ref 4].

## Validation Rules
- **Côte d’Ivoire:** Invoices must be validated by the tax authority before reaching the buyer [Ref 4].

## Tax & VAT Compliance
- **EU (ViDA):** Aims to simplify VAT compliance across EU Member States and reduce VAT fraud through real-time digital reporting [Ref 6].
- **Pakistan:** The reform aims to enhance compliance, enable real-time sales tax reporting, and reduce VAT leakage [Ref 3].
- **Vanuatu:** Implementing e-Invoicing to improve tax compliance and reduce the tax gap [Ref 7].
- **EU (ViDA):** Platforms (e.g., Airbnb, Uber) are deemed to be the supplier for VAT purposes for short-term rentals (max 30 nights) and passenger transport by road, and must collect and remit VAT unless the underlying supplier provides a valid VAT number [Ref 6].

## Submission & Reporting
- **EU (ViDA):** Real-time digital reporting of invoice data to national tax authorities [Ref 6]. Tax authorities will share data via a centralized EU system [Ref 6].
- **Pakistan:** Businesses must complete integration with FBR systems via licensed integrators [Ref 3].
- **North Macedonia:** Real-time invoice data flow through a centralized platform [Ref 12].
- **South Africa:** E-Reporting involves electronically submitting tax data from e-invoices, e-debit notes, and e-credit notes [Ref 9].

## Regulatory Changes (Diff vs. previous)
- **Finland:** By 2030, Finland plans to implement VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) for cross-border transactions [Ref 6].
- **Singapore:** E-invoicing will become mandatory from November 1, 2025, for newly incorporated voluntary GST registrants and from April 1, 2026, for all new voluntary registrants [Ref 6].
- **Hungary:** Effective July 1, 2025, Hungary mandates e-invoicing for B2B transactions involving electricity and natural gas traders [Ref 6].
- **Croatia:** As of January 1, 2026, mandatory e-invoicing will apply to all domestic B2B transactions for VAT-registered businesses [Ref 6]. Non-VAT registered businesses must accept e-invoices from this date and will be required to issue them starting January 1, 2027 [Ref 6].
- **Belgium:** Mandatory B2B e-invoicing for all VAT-registered entities in Belgium, effective from 1 January 2026 [Ref 10].
- **Romania:** Introduction of the FNE (Factura Națională Electronică) and RNE (Registrul Național de Evidență) systems [Ref 11].
- **South Africa:** The draft introduces definitions for electronic invoicing, electronic reporting, and an interoperability framework [Ref 9].

## Implementation Impact
- **Romania:** Businesses should upgrade their accounting systems to support electronic invoicing and train staff on the new systems and compliance requirements [Ref 11].
- **Pakistan:** Companies are advised to assess their categories based on turnover, upgrade their systems for FBR integration, utilize the testing phase to resolve issues, and train staff on new processes [Ref 3].
- **Côte d’Ivoire:** Companies must choose their issuance channels (API, web portal, mobile app, or electronic payment terminals) and ensure compliance through proper data mapping, testing, and stakeholder training [Ref 4].
- **South Africa:** Businesses may expect future regulations detailing e-invoicing frameworks and technical requirements [Ref 9].
- **North Macedonia:** Companies can connect via APIs for integration with existing systems and should prepare for the implementation timeline and adapt their invoicing systems [Ref 12].
